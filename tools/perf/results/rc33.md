# Solar on Floptle 0.117.0-rc33 — trial against rc30 (2026-10-26, 05:45–07:10)

Machine: 16 cores, every run silent (ALSA null), XWayland, vsync Off, one engine at
a time under the shared lock. `load` is `uptime`'s 1-minute average before the run.
The engine developer's builds shared the lock, so the load ran 1–10 (mostly 4–8).
rc33 and rc30 were run back to back (interleaved) for every comparison.

Every run is on a frozen copy of Solar HEAD 73ad62b, through `tools/perf/bench.sh`.
It carries one probe-only patch (`probe33.patch`) against `perfbench.lua`, with three
additions:
- the sleep watch also sums rc33's `perf.counts().wakes` by cause, and prints the share
  of frames awake and the physics over all frames (`RESULT [..] WAKES`);
- `GROUND96` also prints `terrain.stats()` (`TSTATS`);
- park ends with a positive control for the wake counts (`WAKECTL`).

rc30 has no `wakes`; its column reads "n/a". Solar's project.ron was not migrated. In
a scratch copy, `floptle check` gives 0 errors and the usual 6 warnings on both
rc33 and rc30. The luajit sweep ran 81 tests with 0 failures.

## Verdict

**Ready to move to rc33: yes.**
- **Held-force sleep now works for Solar.** With the `parkedStill` gates off, the braked
  rover and the 1,002-part spine stay asleep in the breeze for the whole watch on rc33
  (0 % of frames awake). On rc30 they were awake 97 % and 59 % of frames. **The gates
  can go** (see 1).
- The stutter guard is clean, and the five-mode bench is within spread of rc30. Its one
  outlier, the builder's 1,002-part release, swings the same way on both engines.
- The gameplay smoke matches rc29/rc30: only the two known failures.

One thing does not improve. **Card 0533's fog target is still missed with plants in
view**, and on bare ground it is now missed by a little:
- The two-layer fog march costs 1.2–1.6 ms more than rc30's march.
- The plants save only about 1 ms of their own fog.
- Net with plants: −0.2 ms at 12 steps and −0.2 ms at 16 steps.
- On bare ground, 16 steps went from 3.95 ms to 4.7 ms.

The picture is unchanged.

wind's aero force and torque, the reaction-wheel torque and the anti-roll torque are
then written every tick, as before the gates (the trace confirms 2,700 writes of each
in the 45 s watch). "Awake %" is the share of frames in the watch with the craft
awake. "physics" is the engine's physics bucket over every frame of the watch.

**The wake counts work.** In the positive control, on the sleeping rover:
- a 0.5 m/s kick (`assembly.impulseAt`) read `{impulse=1}` on the next frame and nothing after;
- W read `{joint=5}` (the chassis and its wheels).

In both gates-OFF watches on rc33 the count stayed empty: nothing woke either craft.

**Verdict: drop the gates.**
- rc33's give is a fifth of the weight, and that much torque at the craft's reach. It
  absorbs what Solar's gates were hiding:
  - the breeze, the reaction wheels' damping and the anti-roll bar on the rover;
  - the 7.5–10.8 kN of wind on the 80 t spine.
- Without the gates, rc33 matches the gates-on control to within 0.1 ms. Sleep is just
  as immediate, and the crafts are never woken.
- They are not worth narrowing. The only thing left that they save is about four Lua
  calls a tick on a parked craft (`aeroTick` is 0.04 ms/frame).
- A gust above a fifth of the weight still wakes the craft, as the gates intended.

When Solar moves to rc33, remove:
- `parkedStill`, `PARK*` and `AERO.held` (nothing reads `AERO.held`);
- their three call sites in `vessel_controller.lua` (`wheelTorque`, the anti-roll bar in
  `drive_tick`, the `held` test in `aeroTick`).

`$SCRATCH/sleep/vc_revert.patch` is the full removal against an older HEAD; it needs
rebasing.

## 6. `terrain.stats()` on the home world (rc32+) — the player's save, Zorosia

`TSTATS` reads the sum over the two terrains the save has live. Figures are in MB.

| when | total | meshesGpu | collision | shadow | field | standIns | meshesCpu | tables | dataChunks / standInChunks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| at the reveal (3 loads) | 209–221 | 28–37 | 14–18 | 102.6 | 14–18 | 44.4–44.6 | 0 | 2.2 | 108–150 / 7,600 |
| after 20 s standing + 40 s walking at the spawn (2 loads) | 673–676 | **335** | **110–113** | 102.6 | 63.5 | 41.6 | 17.6 | 2.2 | 562 / 7,132 |
| after the 220 m trip to the meadow + 40 s walk | **800** | **365** | **196** | 102.6 | 76.0 | 40.9 | 17.5 | 2.2 | 667 / 7,011 |

`detail` stayed at 1, and no budget was set.

What it says:
- About half the total is the drawn chunk meshes. `meshesGpu` is "at their capacity",
  so it is the room allocated, not what is drawn.
- The collision surface is the second largest, and it grows with distance travelled:
  110 MB at the spawn, 196 MB after one 220 m trip.
- The volume shadow is a flat 103 MB.
- The voxel field itself is small (64–76 MB).

**Recommendation: don't call `setBudget` yet.**
- 0.8 GB after a short walk is fine on the machines Solar targets.
- A budget only thins far LOD detail. What grows here is the collision surface along
  the player's path. It is not clear the budget releases that, or should.
- Next step: a long rover traverse or a low flight with `TSTATS` every 30 s. If the
  total keeps climbing with distance (collision never released), that is an engine
  question first (see For E 3). Only then is a budget of about 1–1.5 GB worth trying
  as a ceiling the normal game never reaches.

## For E (framed generally)

1. **0533: the two-layer fog march is paid for, but plants still mostly don't read it.**
   - **Measured** (meadow at High, 1920×1052, ~300 instanced alpha-cutout plants in
     view):
     - The shared march pass went from 1.9 to 3.1 ms at 12 steps, and from 2.2–2.3
       to 3.7–3.8 at 16.
     - "Opaque + lighting" with fog fell by only 1.0–1.4 ms. The plants' fragments
       still cost about 6.4 ms of fog there (11.0 ms vs 4.7 with fog off).
     - Net fog cost: 7.7–7.9 ms at 12 steps and 10.3–10.5 at 16, against rc30's 8.0
       and 10.6. With the plants hidden it rose from 3.95 to 4.7 ms at 16 steps, so
       bare ground now misses the 4 ms target.
   - **Ask:** check how many plant fragments take the shared texel vs the per-fragment
     fallback. A debug count, or a view of which path each pixel took, would show
     whether the far-layer match is too strict for cutout leaves, or whether the plants
     are absent from the depth that builds the two layers. And make the second layer
     cheaper where nothing thin stands in front of the ground: a single-layer fast path
     when the near and far depths agree. Any game with grass under volumetric fog hits
     both.
   - **Repro:** `bench.sh <v> gpu` with `BENCH_STRS='("where","walk"),("gfx","High"),("toggles","fogvol0,fogsteps16,flora0,flora0fogvol0,flora0fogsteps16")'`.
2. **Held-force sleep: confirmed fixed (0.2 × weight give).**
   - **Measured:** a braked 0.59 t rover under a 0.17–0.27 kN breeze, plus three small
     stabiliser torques re-written every tick, stayed asleep 45 s (0 % awake,
     0.07 ms/frame). On rc30 it was awake 97 % (0.42 ms). A resting 80 t, 1,002-part
     compound under 7.5–10.8 kN of wind stayed asleep 20 s (1.0 ms/frame). On rc30 it
     was awake 59 % (26.9 ms/frame).
   - Solar can drop its script-side workaround.
   - `perf.counts().wakes` works and is the right tool. A kick read `impulse=1`, a
     drive input `joint=5`, and a quiet park read nothing.
   - One loose end, seen once and not chased: a velocity write
     (`node.vel = node.vel + up*2`) on a sleeping plain prop (a crate) left it asleep
     for the 3 frames watched, with `wakes` empty. The docs say a `node.vel` write
     wakes a body. Worth a two-line test on the engine side; it may be the probe.
3. **`terrain.stats()`: the collision surface grows with distance travelled.** On a
   799 m planet it read:
   - 14–18 MB at the reveal;
   - 110 MB after a minute's walk;
   - 196 MB after one 220 m trip.

   `meshesGpu` (counted at capacity) is 335–365 MB.
   - **Ask:** does `setBudget` (or anything else) ever release the collision surface
     away from every body? Say in the docs which parts of `total` a budget can reclaim.
     Any open-world game that streams collision along the player's path needs to know
     whether that memory is bounded.
4. **Catch-up again.** The builder's 1,002-part release costs 12.2–12.5 ms a tick on
   both engines. Whether a run settles at 1.6 or 3.7 ticks a frame decides whether
   physics is 20 or 45 ms/frame. The catch-up budget asked for before (a
   `tick_budget_ms`-style default) would make that deterministic.
