# Solar on Floptle 0.117.0-rc29 — trial against rc27 (2026-10-26, 01:00–03:30)

Machine: 16 cores, every run silent (ALSA null), XWayland, vsync Off, one engine at
a time under the shared lock. `load` is `uptime`'s 1-minute average before the run.
Three other Solar agents and E's cargo tests shared the lock, so the load ran
4–14. Where a run's load was high enough to skew it (rc29's first base, rocket and
plane runs at load 11–14), it was run again, interleaved with rc27 at load 4–7,
and only the rerun is compared.

Every run is on a frozen copy of Solar HEAD dac5bda (the working tree had other
agents' edits in progress), through `tools/perf/bench.sh` with one probe-only
patch: `perfbench.lua` reads rc28's new `ticks` per frame and prints ticks/frame
and physics per tick, plus fog toggles `fogsteps16`, `flora0fogvol0` and
`flora0fogsteps16`. rc27 has no `ticks`, so its per-tick columns are blank. Solar's
project.ron was not migrated. In the scratch copy, `floptle check` on rc29 gives 0
errors and the usual 6 warnings. luajit sweep: 81 tests, 0 failed.

## Verdict

**Ready to move to rc29: yes.**
- The flat, unlit ground (card 0537) is fixed. Without Solar's slot-keeper
  workaround, the ground was lit and textured on 5 of 5 loads.
- The fog's GPU cost roughly halves.
- Parked craft sleep, and `wake` now names a current cause.
- The stutter guard is clean.
- Every other scenario is within run-to-run spread.

Two things to know:
- Card 0533's 4 ms target is met on bare ground only. Fog over flora is still 7–8 ms.
- rc28's held-force sleep doesn't help Solar on its own: a breeze's force changes
  more than 1 % a tick. **Keep Solar's `parkedStill` gates.**

## 7. `origin_rebase_m` (card 0535)

- **What it is:** the distance the camera may get from the physics' floating origin
  before the origin moves to it. It defaults to 4,096 m when unset, and is read
  before every Play tick (`set_origin_threshold`).
- **Why the default suits Solar:**
  - The planet's own motion never counts toward it. Since rc25 the physics
    frame rides the camera's planet (`PhysicsWorld::ride` moves the origin by the
    planet's carry every tick), so the 1.5–6 m a tick the planet moves does not
    bring the origin any closer to a rebase.
  - Only travel within the planet's frame counts. Zorosia's radius is 799 m, so
    anywhere on the surface or in low flight is within about 1.6 km of a
    base-centred origin; only orbit and long flights pass 4 km.
  - At 4 km out, positions step in about 0.25 mm. That is far finer than
    anything Solar's contacts or sleep thresholds need (0.83 mm a tick).
- **Recommendation: leave it at the default.** A smaller value would rebase more
  often and buy precision nothing in Solar needs. Revisit it only if a far-out craft
  (orbit, other bodies' surfaces) ever shows contact jitter.

## For E (framed generally)

1. **0533: plants do not read the shared fog march.** On a meadow at High, the fog
   is 3.1 ms at 12 steps and 3.8 ms at 16 with the plants hidden, which meets the
   card's target. With ~300 instanced alpha-cutout plants in view it is 6.8–8.0 and
   9.5–11.1 ms. The extra cost is in "opaque + lighting", so the plants' fragments
   take the per-fragment fallback (no fog texel at their depth). They are likely
   left out of the depth prepass, or rejected by the four-texel depth check at
   cutout edges.
   - **Ask:** let alpha-cutout and instanced scatter read the shared march. Put
     cutout geometry in the prepass, or use a depth-tolerant, nearest-depth
     upsample instead of the strict four-texel check. Any game with grass or
     foliage under volumetric fog hits this.
   - **Repro:** `bench.sh <v> gpu` with
     `BENCH_STRS='("where","walk"),("gfx","High"),("toggles","flora0,flora0fogvol0,fogvol0")'`.
2. **Held-force sleep: the 1 % tolerance is too tight for real forces.** A breeze,
   a buoyancy force or a stabiliser's torque varies by more than 1 % every tick
   (here 7.5–10.7 kN on a craft at rest). The body sleeps, then the next tick's
   change wakes it.
   - **Measured:** a resting 80 t compound was awake on 150 of 242 frames
     (27.8 ms/frame vs 1 ms asleep). A braked rover was awake on 97 % of frames.
   - **Ask:** judge a held force against what the support can absorb, not against
     the force the body fell asleep under. For example, stay asleep while the
     change in force is under a fraction of the body's weight (or its static
     friction). The motion test still wakes it if it actually moves.
   - **Repro:** a body at rest with `applyForce` of (1 ± 0.2) × 1 % of its weight,
     re-randomised every tick.
3. **`wake` hides a force wake that repeats every tick.** On that rover the readout
   was `settling` on 100 % of frames while the rover was awake on 97 % of them, so
   it never named the force changes that kept waking it. The spine read `force`
   correctly.
   - **Ask:** have `wake` report the last waker while the body is awake, or add a
     per-frame count of re-wakes by cause. Then "falls asleep and is woken every
     tick" can be told from "never stops moving".
4. **`ticks` per frame works and made the catch-up readable.**
   - The awake 1,002-part craft costs 7.3 ms a tick, the same as on rc27.
   - The builder's 1,002-part release runs 7.65 of the 8 allowed ticks per frame,
     at 12.2 ms each (94 ms/frame).
   - A long frame on a loaded machine spirals: one take-off at load 14 went to
     4.2 ticks/frame and 31 ms of physics, against 1.4 and 6 at load 5.
   - The catch-up budget asked about earlier (a `tick_budget_ms`-style default)
     would bound this.
5. **0537 confirmed fixed.** It also halved fieldEdited (194 → 97) and
   fieldStandIns (14.7k → 7.0k) at the same spot. rc27 was counting the destroyed
   terrain's field alongside the live one.
